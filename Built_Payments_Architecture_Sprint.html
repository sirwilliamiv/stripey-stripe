00

Engagement context

Built connects lenders, owners, developers, general contractors, and subcontractors around the construction loan lifecycle — draws, inspections, lien waivers, and payments. Money movement is core to the product, but today’s payment rails are provided through a third-party B2B payments partner. This sprint evaluates re-platforming that money movement onto Stripe to own the economics, accelerate draws, and embed financial accounts and cards directly in Built.

What Built does today (public)

The system of record for construction finance

~10% of US construction spend and $300B+ of projects run through the platform: loan administration, draw management, inspections, lien-waiver and compliance workflows, contractor invoicing, and an AI Draw Agent that automates draw review.

Why revisit the payment layer

Own the money, not just the workflow

Payments routed through a partner means thin economics, limited control over fund timing, and a ceiling on embedded-finance products (accounts, cards, financing). Owning the rails turns a cost line into a revenue line and unlocks faster, conditional disbursement.

What this sprint delivers

  • A target architecture mapping Built’s money flows to a specific Stripe product stack, with rationale.
  • The draw-disbursement money-flow model — including escrow-style holds, retainage, and lien-waiver gating.
  • The decisions that carry liability — Connect model, fund custody, licensing posture, onboarding, tax.
  • A risk register of the failure modes that actually bite in production money movement.
  • A phased roadmap and an illustrative business case sized to justify the build.
01

Current state & the problem to solve

Construction payments are unusually hard: funds originate from a lender’s loan, flow down a chain of GCs and subs, and every release is conditional on inspections and signed lien waivers. Retainage (a withheld percentage) is held until completion. Payees are thousands of small contractors who must be onboarded, verified, and 1099-reported. The architecture has to model conditional, multi-party, partially-held money — not a simple charge.

Constraints to honor

Non-negotiables from the domain

Funds must not release until a lien waiver is executed and inspection passes; retainage must be withheld and released later; joint-check / dual-obligee scenarios exist; full auditability is required (Built runs SOC 1 / SOC 2 Type II).

Target outcomes

What “better” looks like

Same-day disbursement to subs; embedded financial accounts per project; virtual cards for material purchases against an approved draw; programmatic release driven by the AI Draw Agent; captured payments economics; clean 1099 reporting.

02

Target architecture — the Stripe stack

Built becomes a Connect platform. Lenders, GCs, and subs are connected accounts. Per-project Treasury financial accounts hold committed draw funds and enforce conditional release. Issuing turns approved draws into spend-controlled cards. Enablers (Identity, Financial Connections, Radar, Tax) run across the whole flow.

Connect
The spine. Orchestrates multi-party money movement and onboards lenders, GCs, and subcontractors as connected accounts with their own payout rails and KYC state.
Treasury
A financial account per project (or per loan) holds the funded draw. Funds sit “committed but undisbursed,” enabling escrow-style conditional release and retainage holds rather than instant pass-through.
Issuing
Virtual (and physical) cards issued against an approved draw line, with spend controls tied to budget categories — so a GC can buy materials without a separate cash advance.
Global Payouts
Pays subs and vendors via ACH or instant rails from the project account, domestically and cross-border for suppliers.
Identity   Financial Connections
KYB/KYC for contractor onboarding and bank-account verification — both to fund accounts (debits) and to pay out to verified accounts (anti-fraud).
Radar
Screens inbound funding and — critically here — payout/redirect fraud, where a bad actor diverts a subcontractor payment.
Tax (1099)   Sigma
Connect tax reporting generates contractor 1099s; Sigma/Data Pipeline feed reconciliation, audit, and the portfolio analytics Built already sells.
Agentic / programmatic
The AI Draw Agent calls the transfer/payout APIs to release funds the moment conditions are met — with human-in-the-loop controls and hard limits.

04

The decisions that carry liability

These are the choices a senior architect is actually paid to make. Each one shifts where risk, money, and regulatory burden sit.

Fund-flow model: hold-then-transfer, not pass-through

Recommendation · Treasury account + separate charges & transfers

Because release is conditional and retainage must be withheld, funds cannot simply pass through on receipt. Capital is funded into a held Treasury balance per project; transfers to GC/sub connected accounts are issued only when the gate clears. This is what makes “escrow-like” behavior real instead of a UI illusion.

Custody & licensing posture: keep Built off the money-transmission hook

Recommendation · Stripe (and its bank partners) as regulated party

Holding and moving other parties’ funds raises money-transmission and custody questions. Routing custody through Stripe Connect + Treasury keeps the regulated, licensed entity outside Built wherever possible — a primary reason to buy rather than build. This posture must be confirmed with counsel before launch; the architecture is designed to support that conclusion, not to assert it.

Onboarding: progressive KYB for thousands of small subs

Recommendation · Connect onboarding + Identity, tiered by payout size

The biggest adoption risk isn’t code — it’s friction. Collect the minimum to receive a first payout, then escalate verification as cumulative payouts cross thresholds. Pre-verify bank accounts with Financial Connections to cut the dominant construction fraud vector: redirected payouts.

Cards vs cash: Issuing for material spend

Recommendation · virtual cards against the approved draw line

Issuing spend-controlled cards against a draw category lets a GC buy materials immediately, keeps the spend inside Built’s ledger, and earns interchange — converting a payments cost center into margin while improving the contractor’s cash timing.

Tax: treat 1099 reporting as a first-class output

Recommendation · Connect tax reporting from day one

Construction generates heavy 1099-NEC volume. Wiring tax reporting in at the start (not bolting it on later) avoids a painful year-end retrofit and is a tangible selling point to lenders and GCs.

05

Risk register — the production landmines

The failure modes that separate a demo from a system that moves real money safely. Each is paired with the mitigation baked into the architecture.

Webhook-driven releaseHigh

Risk: releasing funds off a UI action or a browser redirect that can be replayed, dropped, or reordered. Mitigation: every disbursement is triggered only by signature-verified, idempotent webhook handlers; the ledger — not the UI — is the source of truth.

Double disbursementHigh

Risk: a retried request pays a sub twice — unrecoverable once funds leave. Mitigation: idempotency keys on every transfer/payout, keyed to the draw line item.

Payout redirection fraudHigh

Risk: a bad actor changes a subcontractor’s bank details and diverts a payment — endemic in construction. Mitigation: Financial Connections bank verification, change-of-bank cooldowns and re-verification, Radar signals on payout anomalies.

Negative balances & clawbacksMedium

Risk: a reversed funding or disputed draw leaves a connected account negative. Mitigation: hold-then-transfer model limits exposure; define negative-balance liability and top-up flows in the Connect configuration up front.

Reconciliation driftMedium

Risk: balances that don’t tie out across Treasury, transfers, retainage, and the loan ledger. Mitigation: integer minor-unit math, a double-entry internal ledger reconciled to Stripe via Data Pipeline, daily tie-out reports in Sigma.

API version driftMedium

Risk: a Stripe version upgrade silently changes behavior in a money path. Mitigation: pin the API version, gate upgrades behind a replay test suite covering refunds, retainage release, disputes, and partial draws.
